# Q1718: a curve-preserving homeomorphism can fail differentiability on a Cantor set, but only on a null set

## Source and answers

Katherine Williams Booth, *A local characterization for homeomorphisms that preserve continuously differentiable curves*, [arXiv:2410.21460v4, October 7, 2026, Question 1.5, printed/PDF p. 3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.21460v4#page=3), asks whether the set of nondifferentiability points of a member of Homeo¹₁(S) can “be uncountable? Or have positive measure?” Its elements and their inverses take each properly embedded regular C¹ circle to such a circle, as an unparametrized submanifold. The earlier author dissertation poses the same branches as [§1.6, Question 3](https://hdl.handle.net/1853/77809).

**Answers.** Uncountable, yes: on any smooth surface there is a disk-supported, locally bilipschitz member with nondifferentiability set exactly a Cantor set. Positive area, no: every surface homeomorphism taking regular C¹ circles to regular C¹ circles is differentiable almost everywhere. The negative answer does not even require preservation by the inverse.

## Proof

**Almost-everywhere differentiability.** Work in a small source coordinate rectangle whose image lies in a target chart. A short horizontal or vertical closed segment can be included in a smooth embedded circle in the source chart by rounding a closure away from the segment. Its image is therefore a subarc of a regular C¹ curve. Locally that curve is a C¹ graph over some axis. Along the input segment, its coordinate on this axis is a continuous strictly monotone function of the input parameter, since both maps are injective on the arc. This function is differentiable with finite derivative almost everywhere. The chain rule with the C¹ graph makes the vector-valued restriction differentiable almost everywhere on that subinterval. A countable cover yields the conclusion along every coordinate segment. Fubini applied to measurable coordinate difference quotients gives finite partial derivatives in both coordinate directions almost everywhere. The [Gehring–Lehto differentiability theorem](https://data.nationallibrary.fi/bib/me/W00551526000) for continuous open planar maps then implies total differentiability almost everywhere. Apply countably many coordinate rectangles. 

**The Cantor construction.** Work in a planar chart and start with a closed disk D₀ of radius r₀. Each level-(n−1) core disk, centered on the horizontal axis, contains two disjoint closed support disks of radius Rₙ=2^(−n−10)rₙ₋₁, centered at offsets ±rₙ₋₁/8 along that axis. Each support disk has a concentric closed core of radius rₙ=Rₙ exp(−Lₙ), with Lₙ chosen below. Write Kₙ for the union of the 2ⁿ cores and K for their nested intersection. Binary addresses identify K with {0,1}^ℕ. It is contained in the horizontal axis.

Let sₙ=2 for odd n and 1/2 for even n, and Aₙ=s₁…sₙ, with A₀=1. Choose smooth φ:ℝ→[0,1] equal to one on (−∞,0] and zero on [1,∞). On a level-n support disk around c, let gₙ(c+v)=c+aₙ(|v|)v, where aₙ(r)=exp((log sₙ)φ(log(r/rₙ)/Lₙ)), extended by sₙ at zero. Elsewhere gₙ is the identity. This matches smoothly at the two radii. Its derivative has tangential value aₙ(r) and radial value aₙ(r)+raₙ′(r). Choose Lₙ large enough that Aₙ₋₁|raₙ′(r)|≤2^(−n−3) everywhere. Thus gₙ is a smooth radial diffeomorphism, preserving the support disk and dilating its core by sₙ.

Define Fₙ=g₁∘⋯∘gₙ with F₀ the identity. Inductively Fₙ restricts to a positive scalar similarity with derivative Aₙ I on each level-n core. Indeed gₙ preserves its support disk within the level-(n−1) core, where Fₙ₋₁ is such a similarity. At any point a finite-stage derivative is Aⱼ₋₁Dgⱼ from the first level j at which the point leaves the cores, or Aₙ I if it stays through level n. Outside D₀ it is I. Later maps are the identity near any point outside the closed cores of its first departure level. At a core boundary the earlier formula agrees with the scalar similarity, and all later supports remain strictly inside. This justifies the derivative formula at every point, not just almost everywhere. Thus every derivative equals bI+E with 1≤b≤2 and ||E||≤1/8. On a level-N core for n≥N the stronger bound ||E||≤δ_N=2^(−N−3) holds. All these matrices are symmetric. Integrating on line segments gives uniform bilipschitz bounds, using the lower bound on the scalar product with the segment vector. Also ||Fₙ−Fₙ₋₁||∞≤4Rₙ since gₙ moves points only inside the support disks and Fₙ₋₁ is a similarity there. Hence Fₙ converges uniformly. The limit F is bilipschitz onto the plane, by the uniform bounds and compactness of the preimages of a fixed point under Fₙ, which stay in D₀ for points in D₀. It is smooth with smooth inverse locally off K and F(K). Later domain compositions preserve each level-N core as a set, so all Fₙ for n≥N and F send that core onto the same round disk F_N(D).

**Why both maps preserve C¹ curves.** On any fixed level-N core, integration on a straight segment and passage to the uniform limit give
F(y)−F(x)=b(x,y)(y−x)+E(x,y), with 1≤b(x,y)≤2 and |E(x,y)|≤δ_N|y−x|.
The scalar can be selected by a convergent subsequence for each pair. At p∈K every core containing p contains a neighborhood of p. Therefore all secant directions near p change by a quantity tending uniformly to zero. More explicitly, any matrix bI+E in the estimate sends a unit vector to within δ_N of b times itself; as b≥1, normalizing changes its direction by at most 2δ_N/(1−δ_N). For the inverse, the same assertion follows by integrating derivatives of Fₙ⁻¹ on the common image disk F_N(D), then taking the uniform limit of the inverses. Their matrices there are (bI+E)⁻¹, which differ from b⁻¹I by O(δ_N), uniformly. The inverse convergence follows from the uniform lower Lipschitz bound for the Fₙ.

For a regular C¹ input arc near p∈K, write it as a graph over its tangent axis, and restrict to a short segment on which oriented secants lie in a narrow cone about that axis. The preceding estimate for sufficiently large N makes the image projection strictly monotone, so the image is locally a graph. At image points of K, taking the two secant endpoints in successively deeper cores shows that actual image slopes tend to the source tangent slope, in the same graph coordinates. Since the image projection is a continuous strictly monotone parameter, this proves existence of the derivative of the image graph there. At the other points smoothness gives the derivative. These derivatives vary continuously at an image point of K, since source tangent directions vary continuously and the secant estimate controls the change of direction on arbitrarily small cores. This proves the graph is regular C¹ there. Off K the smooth diffeomorphism argument applies. Repeat for F⁻¹ using its estimates, and extend both maps by the identity outside the chart.

**Every point of K is nondifferentiable.** For p∈K in the level-n core D, use qₙ=p+(rₙ/2)e₂, where e₂ is vertical. The child centers and supports are within rₙ/4 of D's center, so p is within rₙ/4 of it, qₙ lies in D, and qₙ is outside all next-level support disks since p lies on the horizontal axis. Thus F(qₙ)=Fₙ(qₙ). For p, every later composition keeps it in the level-(n+1) support disk that contains it, so |F(p)−Fₙ(p)|≤4Rₙ₊₁=o(rₙ). It follows that (F(qₙ)−F(p))/(rₙ/2)=Aₙe₂+o(1). The two subsequential limits e₂ and 2e₂ exclude even the vertical directional derivative. This is the entire nondifferentiability set since F is smooth off K.

The argument uses ordinary Fréchet differentiability in smooth local coordinates; this notion and area-nullness do not depend on the chosen smooth atlas. Curve preservation concerns the set of image points and regular local graph parametrizations. It does not assume a C¹ parametrization is preserved with its original speed.
